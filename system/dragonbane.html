What is this system

A reimagining of the classic Swedish RPG 'Drakar och Demoner' (1982). A light, fast fantasy system: roll d20, get under your skill. Combat on initiative cards, magic with misfire risk, skills that grow from Dragons and Demons. 'D&D, if the Swedes made it in 2023.'

Is it for your table?
Pick it if
  • you want light, fast fantasy you can start straight from the box
  • your table likes card-drawn initiative and roll-under skill checks
  • you want bright fantasy with dark undertones — and duck-folk heroes
Skip it if
  • you want deep character builds and long spell lists
  • your group dislikes magic that can blow up in the caster's face
Setting

Bright fantasy with dark undertones. Dragons, knights, dungeons — with a Nordic flavor. Six kin: humans, halflings, dwarves, elves, mallards (duck-folk) and wolfkin. The core box includes a map of the Misty Vale region and an adventure book whose adventures link into a campaign.

What it looks like at the table
Initiative cards are dealt. You're a duck knight with sword and shield. Your card is 3, the goblin's is 7. You strike first! Swords roll: d20, need 13 or under. Rolled a 1 — a Dragon, the critical success! You pick a bonus: extra damage. The goblin falls. But the mage's spell roll comes up 20 — a Demon: roll on the magical mishap table, and his staff explodes. Your Dragon lets you mark Swords — at session's end you roll to improve it.

Key mechanics

Card initiative

Every round — a new order by cards. Tactics shift on the fly, combat never becomes routine.

Risky magic

Spells cost Willpower and need a skill roll. Roll a Demon (20) and you suffer a magical mishap. The consequences can be worse than the enemy.

Growth from Dragons and Demons

Roll a Dragon (1) or a Demon (20) and you mark that skill; end-of-session questions earn more marks. Then roll d20 for each marked skill — beat its value and it improves.

Best starter set

Rulebook, adventure book, map, cards, cardboard standees, dice — all in the box. Open it and play.
