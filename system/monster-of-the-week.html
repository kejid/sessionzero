What is this system

Monster of the Week is an action-horror Powered by the Apocalypse game by Michael Sands, published by Evil Hat. The players are a team of hunters in the vein of Buffy, Supernatural and The X-Files: every session is a self-contained episode where you crack a mystery, find the creature's weakness and take it down. Roll 2d6 plus a rating, pick an archetype playbook from the Chosen to the Mundane, and let the Keeper build each mystery from a clear template.

Setting

Our world, today — just with monsters in the shadows. Vampires on the edge of town, ghosts in the old school, cultists on the city council. Your hunters might be small-town teenagers, drifters on a road trip or government agents. You build the setting together: the book hands you tools, not a fixed map.

What it looks like at the table
The mystery's countdown has reached Dusk. Your Spooky investigates the attack site: 2d6+Sharp (+1), rolling 4 and 5 for a 10 — hold 2. "What can hurt it?" Silver and salt, says the Keeper. "Where did it go?" The abandoned ice rink. That night the Mundane goes in swinging: 2d6+Tough (+0), a 3 and a 2 — a miss, mark experience, and the Keeper makes a hard move. So you erase a Luck box and the roll counts as a 12. The monster reels back, but you're one Luck closer to the end, and there's no easy way to earn it back.

Key mechanics

2d6 + Rating

Five ratings — Charm, Cool, Sharp, Tough, Weird. A 10+ is a clean success, 7–9 succeeds at a cost, and 6 or less lets the Keeper make a move while you mark experience.

Investigate a Mystery

The core move: success buys questions from a list — what kind of creature is it, what can hurt it, where did it go. You can't beat the monster head-on until you've found its weakness.

Luck

Seven Luck boxes for a hunter's whole life. Spend one to turn a roll into a 12 or cut incoming harm to zero. Once you're out of Luck, your hunter is truly mortal.

Mystery Countdown

The Keeper builds each mystery from a template: a monster, minions, locations and a countdown from Day to Midnight. Dawdle, and the catastrophe arrives right on schedule.
