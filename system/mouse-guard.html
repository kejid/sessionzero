What is this system

Luke Crane's RPG of David Petersen's Eisner-winning comics is the lightest and most newcomer-friendly game in the Burning Wheel family. You play mice of the Guard, leading patrols between towns, keeping the trails open and saving fellow mice from weather, predators and each other. Tests use a pool of d6s where every 4–6 is a success, and each mouse runs on a Belief, a Goal and an Instinct. The 2015 second edition comes as a boxed set with action cards, dice and a map of the Territories.

Setting

The Mouse Territories, 1152. Mice live in towns hidden among tree roots and under stones, and the Guard of Lockhaven keeps the roads between them alive. Owls, weasels, snakes, snow and floods are more frightening than any dragon, and the turning seasons set the rhythm of a campaign, from spring floods to a starving winter.

What it looks like at the table
Autumn. An owl drops on the patrol and your mouse bolts up an oak trunk. Climbing is in a mouse's Nature, so the GM lets you roll Nature 3 against Obstacle 2. Three dice feel thin, so before rolling you spend a Persona point for +1D. You get 5, 2, 1 and 6: two successes, and the owl's talons rake the bark where your tail was a heartbeat ago.

Key mechanics

Belief, Goal, Instinct

Every mouse carries a Belief, a Goal for this session and an Instinct. Playing to them, or against them, earns Fate and Persona points you later spend to bend the dice.

GM's turn and players' turn

First the GM hands the patrol a mission and throws weather, animals and obstacles at it. Then the players spend the checks they earned on their own business: healing, recruiting allies, chasing personal goals.

Scripted conflicts

Fights, chases and arguments all work the same way: each side secretly scripts three actions (Attack, Defend, Feint, Maneuver) and reveals them one by one, wearing down the other side's disposition.

Failure as a twist

A failed test never stalls the story. The GM either introduces a twist or grants success at the cost of a condition: Hungry and Thirsty, Angry, Tired, Injured or Sick.
