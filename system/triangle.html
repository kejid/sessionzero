What is this system

An RPG about agents of a corporate-paranormal Agency who capture anomalies and lock them in the Vault to keep reality stable. Every agent is built from three parts, the ARC: an Anomaly (the supernatural power bonded to them), a Reality (their life outside work) and a Competency (their job at the Agency). Agency bureaucracy, with its Commendations and Demerits, is an equally dangerous enemy.

Is it for your table?
Pick it if
  • you want weird-fiction missions about agents containing anomalies
  • your group enjoys office satire where the employer is a threat too
  • you prefer light rules focused on investigation and decisions over tactics
Skip it if
  • you want tactical combat or detailed weapons and gear
  • your players dislike morally grey choices with no clear right answer
Setting

The modern world, but behind the facade of reality — anomalies. The Triangle Agency is a corporate paranormal organization that captures them and stores them in its Vault so reality holds together. Every agent carries an anomaly of their own, and between missions they split scarce time between the job, their personal life and those powers — which the Agency frowns on.

What it looks like at the table
Anomaly: a door in an apartment building's basement. It opens into a room that doesn't exist on the building plans. Inside — a chair and a note: 'Sit down.' Roll 6d4 — two threes. Success: the note is in your director's handwriting. But the roll also created Chaos, and the General Manager now has something new to spend. Capture the anomaly by the book, or sit in the chair? Report due by morning.

Key mechanics

ARC: Anomaly, Reality, Competency

Every agent is three choices: an Anomaly (supernatural powers), a Reality (personal life and relationships) and a Competency (their role at the Agency). The publisher counts 729 starting combinations.

Commendations and Demerits

The Agency hands out Commendations for good work and Demerits for violations. Commendations buy perks, even coming back after death; Demerits come with consequences.

Bureaucracy

Reports, clearances, internal politics. The Agency isn't an ally — it's a tool that can turn against you.

6d4 and Chaos

Roll six d4 and count the 3s. Rolls also generate Chaos, the currency the General Manager (the GM) spends on complications. Quality Assurances let you bend the result.
